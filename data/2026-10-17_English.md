  <summary>Details</summary>

Abstract: Thermal confinement is a key parameter in photothermal applications, as it determines the spatial extent of the region affected by the generated heat. The model combines generalized optical heating with a finite-difference solution of the transient heat equation and explicitly accounts for finite interfacial thermal conductance. By comparing the characteristic dimensions of representative targeting ligands with the resulting thermal-confinement length scales, we show how pulse duration can be precisely tailored to control target heating. These results provide a unified framework for predicting nanoscale heat localization paving the way for the rational design of high precision thermoplasmonic applications.

Method: The authors measured drop-size distributions of two full-cone pressure-swirl nozzles by laser diffraction over a range of nozzle-to-beam distances, pressures, and orientations, and used high-speed imaging to observe sheet breakup. Main category: physics.comp-ph

  <summary>Details</summary>
