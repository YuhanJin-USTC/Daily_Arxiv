Abstract: Some turbulent flows self-organize into large-scale structures, rather than breaking up into ever-smaller scales. Underpinning this phenomenon is the existence of two sign-definite quantities which are conserved by the dynamics. Two-dimensional turbulence is a prime example, where large-scale mean flows, termed condensates, spontaneously emerge. We review a perturbative theoretical framework for the statistical description of such inhomogeneous turbulence, offering new perspectives on the role of the two conserved quantities. We illustrate the universal properties of the theory, comparing results from two-dimensional Navier-Stokes to those from the large-scale-quasi-geostrophic equation.
