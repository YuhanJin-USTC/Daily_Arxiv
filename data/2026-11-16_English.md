### [1] [Restoring Consistency with Poynting's Theorem in Numerical MHD and RMHD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18508)
*Dinshaw S. Balsara, Deepak Bhoriya*

Main category: physics.plasm-ph

TL;DR: The paper identifies an inconsistency in MHD and RMHD codes that evolve face-centered magnetic fields in a globally divergence-free way: magnetic fields are advanced using edge-centered electric fields from two-dimensional Riemann solvers, but the face-centered Poynting flux in the energy flux is evaluated with one-dimensional Riemann solvers. <details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To restore consistency with Poynting's theorem in numerical MHD and RMHD, particularly because existing divergence-free magnetic-field schemes do not let edge-centered electric fields influence the face-centered Poynting flux; this inconsistency can produce negative pressure at zone centers when magnetic energy greatly exceeds thermal energy.

Method: The authors demonstrate the inconsistency between face-centered magnetic-field evolution and face-centered energy/Poynting-flux evaluation, then restore consistency by evaluating a face-centered Poynting flux using edge-centered variables. They make a simple accuracy-preserving correction to the face-centered flux of energy density. Details of the discretization, implementation, and test suite are not provided in the abstract.

Result: The inconsistency can lead to negative pressure at zone centers in strongly magnetized situations. The proposed correction helps considerably in simulations with very strong magnetic fields. Quantitative accuracy, error, or performance results are not provided in the abstract.

Conclusion: Numerical MHD and RMHD schemes should use edge-centered information to evaluate the face-centered Poynting flux so that the energy update is consistent with Poynting's theorem. A simple accuracy-preserving correction improves robustness in strong-magnetic-field simulations, though broader validation details are not specified.

Abstract: For perfectly conducting plasmas, Poynting's theorem states that the time rate of change of electromagnetic energy density equals the negative of the divergence of the Poynting flux. To the best of our knowledge, this involutive theorem has never been mimetically incorporated in codes. We show that there is an inconsistency in MHD and RMHD codes that evolve face-centered magnetic fields in globally-divergence-free fashion. This is because the face-centered flux of energy density, which incorporates a Poynting vector, is evaluated using one-dimensional Riemann solvers that are applied at face-centers. However, the face-centered magnetic fields are evolved according to edge-centered electric fields that come from two-dimensional Riemann solvers that are applied to the edges of a zone. While edge-centered electric fields are used to evolve the magnetic field, they have no influence on the face-centered Poynting flux. For situations where the magnetic energy is much larger than the thermal energy, this causes a negative pressure to be evaluated at the zone-centers. This paper restores consistency by using edge-centered variables to evaluate a face-centered Poynting flux. A simple accuracy-preserving correction is made to the face-centered flux of energy density. We show that it helps considerably in simulations that have very strong magnetic fields.
